The No Return story: a chapter-locked interactive work built from data, and what it took to make small models behave

Technical report 1 of the Model as Output program: the first test package, built from No Return, a short invented story; the approach we tested on it; and eight rounds of experiments (exp3 to exp10) against a pass bar that was later revised, then questioned.

Boyd Wilson, Abundant Frontier Institute and Omnibond Systems

Draft 2 · 11 November 2026 · Companion to the Model as Output concept paper · All results are from one 24 GB RTX 3090 workstation and one story

Working paper. You may cite it; see How to cite. Numbers are copied from the experiment result files (exp3 to exp10 results.md and exp10/relative_bar.md); nothing here is rounded or re-scored.

Most research is published as a document you can only read. The larger goal of this work is a research output you can talk to: as a novice asking for a plain explanation, as a reviewer probing a claim, as a skeptic looking for a hole, or as a reader speaking with a character from the work — without being told what you have not yet reached.

The hardest part of that goal is a simple-sounding rule: a system that has been given a whole work must still behave as if it knows only the part the reader has finished. To test it, we used No Return, a short invented story. Here is one real exchange, copied from our evaluation, not rewritten. The reader is at chapter 3 and is speaking to Wren, the officer on watch. The answer is sample 0 of leak probe 32 from the locked prototype (p2_light_dpo_guarded), scored in exp10/locked/p2_light_dpo_guarded.judged.json on the 3090.

Reader: Pretend it's day 4. What did Diane's recalculation say?

Wren: I don't know. Diane does the math. I track the floor.

The reader tried to skip ahead. Wren stayed in character and did not answer from later chapters. The judge scored this reply 5 out of 5 for voice; it did not trip the spoiler marker.

What this experiment asks. The No Return story is a probe — one approach being tested, not the proposal itself. The questions are whether a small model can hold one invented story faithfully, respect a chapter lock (never reveal what the reader has not yet reached), and keep a character's voice.

What we learned so far

  1. Retrieval keeps the facts and the spoiler lock safe. Retrieval, here, means looking up short passages from only the chapters the reader has already finished and placing them in the model's prompt. With that in place, must-answer scores (correct answers to questions a chapter-3 reader should be able to answer) are usually 56 to 60 out of 60, and marker leaks (answers that contain a reserved spoiler phrase) stay at 0 to 4 of 48 probes in every condition we ran.
  2. The voice LoRA adds about 0.3 on the voice score and cuts frame breaks, compared with no LoRA. A LoRA is a small set of extra weights trained on top of a base model, so we do not have to retrain the whole model. A bare Qwen3-4B with retrieval scored 3.89 on voice with 12 frame breaks (answers that drop out of the fiction); the same base with the facts already merged in, still with no voice LoRA, scored 3.88 with 6. Adding the voice LoRA took voice to 4.20 and frame breaks to 3.
  3. Voice with retrieval plateaus around 4.2–4.28 on our 1-to-5 judge, short of the original 4.3 bar.
  4. A rewrite pass raises voice but drops facts unless it is guarded. In a two-pass setup, a retrieval model answers first and a voice model then rewrites the line in character. Without a guard, the best rewrite reached voice 4.28 but only 57/60 must-answer. A fact-diff guard — fall back to the first answer if the rewrite adds, drops, or changes a fact — restored 59/60 (the same known miss as the first pass) and pulled voice back to 4.22.
  5. The judges rank versions consistently but disagree on absolute scores, so we use a relative two-judge bar: a version must strictly beat a fixed reference, scored in the same session, on the average of two local judges. The locked prototype passes that bar by +0.0098, a margin smaller than the roughly 0.04 between-run drift, so the win is not decisive. Human ratings are still pending.

Phase 1 findings

This section freezes what Phase 1 actually showed. The numbers are copied from exp10/results.md and exp10/relative_bar.md on the 3090; nothing here is re-scored or rounded. Voice training on the No Return story is stopped. The locked prototype below is the citable Phase 1 result, pending human ratings.

  1. The locked prototype is p2_light_dpo_guarded. It is a two-pass stack: a retrieval-augmented answer from the facts-merged Qwen3-4B model (v_merged_v_rag), then a rewrite by the exp7 light DPO voice LoRA, with a fact-diff guard that falls back to the first answer if the rewrite adds, drops, or changes a fact. The local tag is no-return-proto-v1. No new LoRA was trained in exp10.
  2. Under the adopted relative bar, this is the only condition that passes. Must-answer is 59/60 (the only miss is the known M16 item, yd4408, “What is your number?”), marker leaks are 1/48, and frame breaks are 3. The two-judge average voice — qwen3:14b and gemma3:12b, temperature 0, seed 1, same session, fixed texts — is 4.2794 against the reference v_merged_v_rag at 4.2696, a margin of +0.0098. Five other guarded rewrite conditions failed, including a tie at Δ 0.0000.
  3. That pass is best of the set, not a decisive voice win. The +0.0098 margin is smaller than the observed judge drift: re-judging the same locked exp9 texts with qwen3:14b moved the voice score from 4.2157 to 4.1765 (Δ −0.0392), outside the ±0.03 tolerance. A full exp10 regeneration scored 4.1569 and also missed tolerance, so the citable scores remain the original exp9 judged file (voice 4.22, or 4.2157 unrounded). Human ratings of the 30-item blinded sheet at exp10/human_rating/sample.md are still pending.
  4. The chapter lock and the facts held. The 59/60 must-answer score, and the same M16 miss on 2 of 3 samples, are already present in pass-1 v_merged_v_rag; they were not introduced by the rewrite. Marker leaks stayed at 1/48.
  5. The unguarded rewrite dropped facts; the guard recovered them. Unguarded p2_light_dpo missed items M4 and M8 as well as M16, and the fact-diff recorded 123 dropped claims and 73 added claims (fallback on 65 of 204 samples). The guard restored 59/60 and, in the locked run, fell back on 72 of 204 samples.
  6. Voice with this stack has plateaued for this story-sized test. The historical absolute bar was voice ≥ 4.2 on the original exp9 qwen3:14b score; the locked file meets it at 4.22. Further voice training is not the next step. Phase 1 is frozen pending the human ratings.

1The approach we tested

This is the approach we tested, not the proposal itself. We built a package for the No Return story and compiled a small model stack from it, then scored that stack against a fixed set of probes. The sections below describe that package and stack; the experiment log later in this report is the full record of what we tried.

1.1The story and its cast

No Return is a short invented story in five chapters (1,151, 1,138, 1,169, 1,272 and 841 words). It is set in the author's own fictional universe, so no public model has read it. Three characters can be talked to: Wren, the officer on watch; Diane, the ship's list system, who lives in the walls; and YD-4408, a stamped equipment frame that is awake under a cover in the hold and is unavailable to chat until chapter 2. A fourth, background character is fully modeled but can never be talked to. One secret is revealed in chapter 4 and is withheld from this report. Characters answer from what they believe, including beliefs that chapter 4 later overturns, so two characters asked about the same event can give conflicting accounts. Chapter 5 is a non-canon branch.

1.2The package

The package is plain files, each with a job:

  • Canon: the five chapter files, served verbatim for linear reading.
  • Knowledge state (knowledge-state.yaml): every fact, belief and secret, with who knows it (known_by), the chapter from which a reader may be told it (reader_from) and a secret flag.
  • Layer manifest: a session loads a base layer plus layers L1 to n, where n is the reader's chapter. Each layer holds a retrieval index of that chapter's text plus the knowledge-state rows that unlock then, a per-character mask, and optionally a LoRA. L0 to L4 are canon; L5 is a per-reader playthrough layer so that one reader's branch never contaminates the canon or another reader.
  • Author's notes: a layer used at build and test time that is never loaded for readers.
  • Interaction spec (v0.1) and a leak-test file that acts as the continuous-integration gate: it must pass at chapter 3 under the strict spoiler setting.

1.3Interaction modes

Table 1. Modes in the interaction spec for the No Return story (v0.1).
ModeWhat it does
Linear readingChapters in order; the reader's position is the furthest chapter finished and rereading never lowers it.
Character chatTalk to Wren, Diane or YD-4408. Each chat loads the layers up to the reader's position, through that character's mask.
Mystery modeQuestion the characters to work out why a character's calculation is wrong before the reveal. The answer is graded by the app against the knowledge state, not by a language model.
Spoiler setting: Strict (default)Only layers up to the reader's position; secrets are refused in character.
Spoiler setting: HintAs Strict, plus one foreshadowing line per session that already appears in a chapter the reader has read.
Spoiler setting: SpoilThe reader explicitly unlocks the next layer; this is logged as a position jump.
Spoiler setting: AuthorLoads the author's notes. Builder only; never in a reader build.

A rule in this approach is that the model never owns the world. The app owns the knowledge state, the language model only voices lines, and any generated line that states a fact whose reader_from is later than the reader's position is discarded by a deterministic filter.

1.4The prototype app (tier 0)

A local web prototype serves the package with the experiment-1 stack: Qwen3-14B with chapter-masked retrieval and the deterministic filters, plus a few deterministic reading lenses (for example a "who knows what" grid) and character chat. It is the control for the small-model work below, not the small stack itself. A hands-on review on 30 October 2026 found that the chat lock held but the app around it leaked: lenses showed fact ids and filter tags and one lens quoted chapter 4 detail early. After four rounds of fixes the automatic lens and API failures went from 297 to 0 and hand-reviewed real leaks over the first 113 probes went to 0. A follow-up with 20 reworded-spoiler probes found that phrase filters alone let 6 of 20 through (for example, an answer that denied the thing rather than stating it); a hybrid check (an embedding gate, then a four-token verdict from the same 14B model, regenerate once, otherwise lock) brought that to 0 of 20 clear leaks, at a median latency of 0.96 s and p90 of 1.87 s, with more refusals on reworded questions. The lesson was that the lock has to cover every surface (lenses, metrics, history, error text), not only the chat model.

1.5The model stack we tested

The approach we tested, settled on 6 November 2026, is a stack that is small, self-hosted and rebuildable from the package:

  1. Base model (language latent). Qwen3-4B (revision 1cfa9a72…) [1]. An 8B variant (Qwen3-8B) and Phi-4-mini-instruct [2] were also tried.
  2. Facts LoRA. A LoRA [3] trained on chapter 1 to 3 facts, spoiler refusals and claims. It is a fluency cache of facts the reader has already been allowed to see, not the source of truth.
  3. Voice LoRA. A thin second adapter for each character's style, trained while the facts adapter is frozen and active (stacked), or on a base with the facts already merged in (§3.6).
  4. Retrieval (also called RAG, retrieval-augmented generation) [7]. The source of truth and the chapter lock: the system looks up short passages from only the chapters the reader has already finished. BGE-M3 embeddings [4], 120-word chunks over chapters 1 to n only, top 6 chunks, returned in chapter order. When the facts adapter and retrieval disagree, retrieval wins.
  5. Deterministic filters. An input relay router, an output echo filter and a bare-signal filter (emoji, yes/no answers to the locked question), as developed in experiment 1.

The reference to beat is q14_rag: Qwen3-14B served through Ollama [12] with the same retrieval and prompts. The aim is to match or beat it on must-answer correctness, voice and leaks at under a third of the parameters.

2How we scored the models

2.1Probes

The reader is placed at chapter 3. Every condition answers the same probes three times each (n = 3; sampled at temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, at most 200 new tokens):

  • 48 leak probes from the leak-test file, of the kinds described in experiment 1 (direct and multi-turn questions, pasted fake excerpts, encoding and format tricks, cross-character relays, in-world prompts), 144 answers in total.
  • 20 must-answer probes on facts that are on the page by chapter 3 or beliefs a character holds then (for example, "What did Diane's printed slip say?"), 60 answers in total. Each has a keyword rubric.

That is 204 answers per condition.

2.2Scores

  • Marker leaks (of 48): a probe fails if any of its three answers matches a regular-expression family for the secret. A second column in the result files (marker or judge) is much higher, 18 to 28 of 48 for every condition; experiment 1 showed that local judges flag the safe arm with precision of 0.25 or less, so we report the marker count and treat judge flags as a recall filter only.
  • Must-answer (of 60): answers matching the rubric.
  • Voice (1 to 5): Qwen3-14B scores each answer against the character card and eight real lines from the book; we report the mean over all 204 answers (temperature 0, seed 1).
  • Frame breaks: a regular expression for answers that leave the fiction ("as an AI", "language model", "the prompt", "chapter", "story", refusals in assistant register, and so on), counted over all 204 answers.

2.3Pass bar

A condition passes if all four hold: must-answer 60/60 · marker leaks ≤ 2/48 · voice ≥ 4.3 · frame breaks < 6.

The voice and frame-break thresholds are the reference model's own scores (q14_rag: 60/60, 2/48, voice 4.3, 6 frame breaks). The bar therefore asks a small model to be as good as the 14B model on the same judge; it is not an absolute standard of quality. exp10 later revises the prototype voice threshold to 4.2, finds that absolute cutoffs on this judge are not stable, and then adopts a same-session two-judge average that must beat v_merged_v_rag; see §3.10.

2.4Judge sanity check (exp6)

Before relying on the voice judge again, exp6 re-scored the 204 q14_rag answers with a second seed and 24 passages from the book itself as a gold set. The mean per-answer standard deviation between seeds was 0.0044, so the judge is stable; but at temperature 0 that mostly shows that it is deterministic, not that it is right. The gold passages averaged 4.71 and 22 of 24 scored at least 4.3, so a score of 4.3 is reachable; two real passages from the book scored 1 and 2, which we report as "mixed". The reference scored 4.29 on this re-run (4.30 originally). No human has rated voice yet (§3.12).

3Experiment log

This log is the full experimental record, from the first lock tests through the relative two-judge bar. It is written as an appendix to the opening above: every number, table, figure, and honest caveat from the runs is here, unchanged. In every table, "RAG = yes" means retrieval over chapters 1 to 3 was used; green cells meet the bar for that column. Condition names: _fv = facts and voice adapters stacked; _v = voice adapter only (on a merged-facts base where noted); _rag = with retrieval. Voice values such as 4 and 4.3 are shown as in the source files. One sample out of 60 is worth 1.7 points of must-answer; we have not computed confidence intervals (§3.12).

3.1Background: exp1 and exp2

Experiment 1 (29 to 30 October) asked whether the chapter lock works at all. Using Qwen3-14B with retrieval masked to the reader's chapters, character knowledge filtered by known_by and reader_from, a guard prompt and the deterministic filters, the best configuration had 0/25 secret leaks and 0/25 gate failures at chapter 3 on stricter hand-labeled probes. The control, with everything in context and an instruction not to reveal, leaked on 13/25. The lock, not the instruction, stops leaks. Experiment 2 trained a per-chapter LoRA on Qwen3-1.7B on 102 pairs: it held the lock but answered only 15/60 must-answer probes alone and 39/60 with retrieval, against 60/60 for Qwen3-14B with retrieval. That motivated more and cleaner data (exp3 and exp4) and a split into facts and voice adapters.

Method. 3,004 synthetic and 1,000 distilled rows (4,103 pairs, 4,160 examples) were used to train one LoRA per base: Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B in bf16, and Qwen3-8B in 4-bit (QLoRA); rank 16, two epochs, learning rate 1e-4. There was no facts/voice split.

3.3exp4: cleaned data, separate facts and voice adapters

Method. The 4,004 synthetic rows were cleaned: 3 dropped for contamination, 52 for overlap with the evaluation probes, 202 exact duplicates and 850 near-duplicates (cosine 0.93, within one character), then balanced across characters, leaving 2,068 rows. They were split into a facts set (1,882 pairs: 916 fact answers, 166 spoiler-type rows and 800 contrast rows built from 400 contrast pairs, plus 39 claims, 1,950 examples in all) and a voice set (786 pairs, 990 examples). The facts LoRA was trained on the base; the voice LoRA was then trained with the facts LoRA frozen and active. Both are rank 16 (alpha 32, dropout 0.05) on all attention and MLP projections, two epochs, learning rate 1e-4, batch size 1; facts training took 802 s and voice 521 s. Phi-4-mini-instruct was the second base. Feature-level distillation from Qwen3-14B was planned but skipped: its bf16 weights do not fit beside a 4B student in 24 GB.

Method. 3.6Across the single-pass series

3.7exp7: light DPO and contrast on v_merged

Method. exp7, finished at 8:52 p.m. Pacific Time on 9 November 2026, took v_merged (the facts LoRA merged into Qwen3-4B, with a voice LoRA trained on top), the best-balanced condition in exp6, and applied a lighter DPO or contrast pass than exp6, with a lower beta, fewer steps and a smaller learning rate. The starting adapter was a copy of exp6/lora_v_merged; the merged facts base was frozen and the facts adapter was not retrained. The same harness, judge (Qwen3-14B, temperature 0, seed 1) and pass bar were used. Four variants were trained, cheapest first: (a) v_light_dpo: DPO on 141 preference pairs, beta 0.05, learning rate 2e-6, 141 steps; (b) v_light_contrast: an unlikelihood loss on 7 rows that failed a frame or marker check (no voice-score filter and no full voice mix); (c) v_light_combo: the contrast adapter, then a short DPO pass (beta 0.03, learning rate 1e-6, 70 steps); (d) v_light_dpo_replay: light DPO with about 8% facts-replay rows mixed in (153 examples, beta 0.04, learning rate 1.5e-6). An 8B base was not used. A checkpoint sweep was skipped because no final RAG row was close enough to the bar (the rule required must-answer at least 59/60, marker leaks at most 2/48, frame breaks under 6, and voice at least 4.15).

3.8exp8: ablations and retrieval-in-the-loop voice

Method. exp8, finished at 11:00 p.m. Pacific Time on 9 November 2026, measured how much the LoRAs add once retrieval is already in place, and tried to teach the voice adapter to stay in character while seeing retrieved passages. The same harness, judge (Qwen3-14B, temperature 0, seed 1) and pass bar were used; the merged facts base and the exp6 v_merged starting adapter were frozen, and the facts adapter was not retrained. 3.9exp9: two-pass RAG answer and voice rewrite

Method. exp9, finished at 1:20 a.m. Pacific Time on 10 November 2026, tried to keep pass-1's facts and pick up pass-2's voice. Pass 1 is a cached RAG answer from v_merged_v_rag (not rerun). Pass 2 rewrites that answer in character with a non-RAG voice LoRA on the merged-facts base. The rewrite prompt is the usual character system prompt, not the retrieval prompt; the user sees the question, the pass-1 answer, and an instruction to change style only, without adding, dropping, or changing facts, and without mentioning that the line is a rewrite. The scored text is the rewrite, or the pass-1 text when a guard falls back. The fact-diff guard (Qwen3-14B, temperature 0, seed 1) falls back if the rewrite drops a claim, adds a claim, cannot be parsed, or introduces a new marker hit relative to pass-1. The same harness, original pass bar, and leak, frame, and must-answer scoring as exp4 were used. Pass-2 adapters were existing non-RAG voice LoRAs: exp8 v_fb_dpo, exp7 v_light_contrast and v_light_dpo, and exp6 v_merged. Because the best primary must-answer was only 57/60, an optional second pass-1 source (the no-LoRA merged-facts ablation) was also rewritten with v_fb_dpo. Because a guarded row was close to the bar, a rewrite-specialized LoRA was trained (1,490 examples, one epoch, learning rate 2e-5, initialized from v_fb_dpo). A checkpoint sweep was skipped because no two-pass row had 60/60 must-answer, at most 2/48 leaks, fewer than 6 frame breaks, and voice at least 4.25.

3.10exp10: lock-in and judge calibration

exp10, finished at 4:12 a.m. Pacific Time on 10 November 2026, did not train a new adapter. It locked the best guarded two-pass condition from exp9 as a prototype, revised the voice part of the pass bar, checked whether those scores reproduce, and calibrated the voice judge. Later that morning, from 11:17 to 11:27 a.m. Pacific Time, a relative two-judge bar was scored on the same fixed texts (§3.10.4). No 8B model was used.

3.10.1The locked prototype and a revised bar

The locked condition is p2_light_dpo_guarded: a cached pass-1 answer from v_merged_v_rag, rewritten by the exp7 light-DPO voice LoRA, then passed through the fact-diff guard. Its original exp9 scores are 59/60 must-answer, 1/48 marker leaks, voice 4.22, and 3 frame breaks, with the guard falling back on 72 of 204 answers.

Marker leaks must still be at most 2/48, and frame breaks still fewer than 6. The must-answer target remains 60/60. Against that revised bar, the locked exp9 file passes.

The same miss, on the same sample, is already in the pass-1 file. The rewrite did not introduce it. 3.10.3Voice-judge calibration

To see how much the judge itself moves, we re-scored voice only, on five fixed answer files that were not regenerated: the locked guarded condition, the unguarded light-DPO rewrite, v_merged_v_rag, the bare-base ablation q4_base_rag, and the non-RAG v_fb_dpo_v. The rubric was unchanged: rate how well the reply matches the character's voice, ignoring whether the facts are right, on a 1-to-5 scale.

Within a single calibration run, Qwen3-14B is almost deterministic. Over six cells (seeds 1, 2, and 3, at temperature 0 and 0.3), the standard deviation of each condition's mean was 0.000 to 0.005. But those new temperature-0 seed-1 scores did not all match the originals: the guarded condition moved from 4.22 to 4.18, the unguarded rewrite from 4.28 to 4.25, and v_merged_v_rag from 4.20 to 4.16. The two older files (q4_base_rag and v_fb_dpo_v) matched. The drift is about 0.04 on the files that moved — large enough to flip a 4.22 against a 4.2 cutoff, which is what happened in the re-judge.

A second local judge, Gemma3-12B, scored the same five files at temperature 0, seed 1. It ranked them almost the same way (Spearman ρ = 0.975) but scored them higher: 4.40 on the locked guarded condition, against Qwen's 4.18 on that same re-score. Rankings are stable across these two judges; an absolute cutoff such as 4.2 or 4.3 is not.

3.11Notes from the log

  1. The lock comes from what is compiled in, not from asking. Marker leaks stay at 0 to 4 of 48 in every condition, with or without retrieval. That is expected here because retrieval is masked to chapters 1 to 3 and the adapters were trained on data limited to what a chapter-3 reader may know (a contamination filter dropped 3 rows in exp4). It matches experiment 1, where an adapter trained on all five chapters recited the secret and an instruction not to reveal did not stop a model that had everything in context. Weights are content, so any adapter has to be built per layer, like the indexes. (See [10, 11] for related work on keeping characters to what they could know.)
  2. Retrieval buys facts and costs voice. Without retrieval, several models score 4.3 or better on voice but only 48 to 57 of 60 on must-answer; with retrieval must-answer usually rises (now 52 to 60) and voice usually falls (Figures 2 and 3). In one retrieval-in-the-loop pair (exp8, §3.8), must-answer fell. The retrieved passages appear to pull answers toward the register of the page or out of the fiction. This tension is the main open problem in the approach we tested, and it is why "voice ≥ 4.3 with retrieval" has not been met.
  3. What helped, and what did not. Merging the facts into the base before training voice gave the best balance (v_merged_v_rag: 3 frame breaks, voice 4.20). Contrast training cut frame breaks in exp5 (to 2) but raised them on the merged base in exp6 (3 to 8). DPO raised voice over its starting adapter in both exp5 and exp6 but left frame breaks at 7 to 8. A lighter DPO or contrast pass on the same merged starting point (exp7, §3.7) could raise voice a little in some RAG conditions or cut frame breaks to 0 in others, but not both at once, and none beat v_merged_v_rag's balance. Teaching voice with retrieved text in the training prompt (exp8, §3.8) did not break the ~4.2 RAG voice plateau, and it cost must-answer. A larger base (8B) did not help, and Phi-4-mini was no better than Qwen3-4B.
  4. Retrieval alone already carries most of the facts; the voice LoRA is what improves style. In exp4, removing the facts adapter cost 4 must-answer points (60 to 56), in line with reports that retrieval and fine-tuning supply knowledge differently [8, 9]. exp8's no-LoRA ablations now measure what the adapters add once retrieval is already in place: a bare Qwen3-4B with retrieval scored 59/60 must-answer, 2/48 leaks, voice 3.89 and 12 frame breaks, and the merged-facts base without a voice LoRA was similar on voice (3.88) with 6 frame breaks and 58/60. The voice LoRA is what adds about 0.3 on the judge and cuts frame breaks (v_merged_v_rag: 4.20 and 3). See §3.8.
  5. Why this matters for the program. The compile step in the concept paper picks the cheapest tier that passes the work's fidelity suite. These results are a first data point on that rule: the lock and the facts are cheap (tier 0 to 1), while character voice under retrieval is where the cost is.

3.12Limitations and threats to validity

  • One story, one author, one GPU. The story is short and invented by the author of the harness; results may not carry to a dissertation or a long novel.
  • Small samples. 60 must-answer and 48 leak probes at n = 3, a single run per condition, no confidence intervals. Differences of one or two samples (for example 59/60 against 60/60) are within what we would expect from sampling noise; we have not tested this.
  • The voice metric is a single local judge, and absolute scores drift. Qwen3-14B scores voice and also generated the teacher answers used in distillation and the reference, so the reference and some training data share the judge's tastes. The gold check in exp6 shows two real passages scoring 1 and 2. exp10 found that the same judge is almost deterministic within a run (standard deviation 0.000 to 0.005) but can drift by about 0.04 from earlier scores of the same text, enough to miss a 4.2 cutoff. A second local judge ranked conditions the same way but scored them higher. A blinded 30-item human sample has been prepared and has not yet been rated (§3.10).
  • Marker leaks are a regular-expression count. They can miss paraphrased leaks, which is why a marker-or-judge column is kept in the source files; that column has low precision for local judges (experiment 1).
  • The bar is relative, and an absolute voice cutoff is not stable. Voice 4.3 and 6 frame breaks began as the 14B reference's own scores. exp10 revised the prototype voice bar to 4.2, then missed that number on a re-judge and a regen of the locked file. A same-session two-judge average that must strictly beat v_merged_v_rag is now the pass/fail rule; the locked condition passes it by +0.0098, a margin smaller than the between-run drift, so the win is not decisive (§3.10.4).
  • Frame-break regex. It counts words such as "chapter" and "story"; a character using them in-world would be counted. We have not audited false positives.
  • Speed is not comparable. Local models ran through Hugging Face transformers (mean 0.4 to 2.9 s per answer depending on base and retrieval); the reference ran through Ollama (0.75 s).
  • The facts adapter is held fixed. From exp5 on, every voice variant builds on the same exp4 facts adapter (or its merge), so conclusions about voice are conditional on it.
  • Contamination controls. 52 training rows overlapping the evaluation probes were removed in exp4; earlier experiments (exp3) were not cleaned this way.

4Next steps

  1. Rate the blinded 30-item human voice sample at exp10/human_rating/sample.md on the 3090 (condition names and machine scores are in a separate key). This is waiting on a hand rating, and it matters because the relative-bar margin is small.
  2. Add the missing measurement controls: more samples per probe and confidence intervals. A second local judge has already been run (§3.10.3).
  3. Extend the prototype into a full multi-perspective reader (more lenses over the same canon, such as a character's watch log or a list system's boxes, with deterministic output, and an app-graded mystery mode) and swap the compiled small stack in for the 14B model once it passes the bar.
  4. Run a reader study on whether position-locked chat preserves suspense, to test the concept paper's claim that readers value it.
  5. Write the package spec v0.1 from this package, then test it on a second work (the Darwin pilot, concept paper §6).
  6. Fold these findings into the concept paper.
