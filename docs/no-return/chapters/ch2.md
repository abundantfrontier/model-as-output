# Chapter 2 — Under the Cover

*Day 2. Layer 2.*

The sound came back on day two, at the same hour. It was not a crate settling. Crates do not settle twice in the same way. It was something sliding, then stopping, then sliding.

**Wren:** Diane. Hold.

**Diane:** The hold is to list, Officer Wren.

**Wren:** I'm going to look at the hold.

**Diane:** Looking at the hold is not on the watch list.

**Wren:** It's on mine.

They took the small lamp from the airlock bracket. The hold lights were on a switch by the hatch, and the switch had a sticker on it in somebody's careful print: BIG LIGHTS — ARRIVAL ONLY. Wren did not know why the big lights were for arrival only. They knew a sticker when they saw one, and a sticker was a person asking. They left the big lights off.

The hold was dark and cold at the edges, warmer in the middle. The small lamp made a circle about as wide as a kitchen table.

The first stack was drums. Four of them, heavy, with nothing written on them. Wren tapped one. It sounded full. Water, probably. That matched the list.

The second stack was racks. Empty racks, tall, the kind you could see straight through. Wren put the lamp behind them and the light came out the other side with nothing in it. Empty racks were not on the list Wren had seen. They were not against it either. Nobody writes *empty* on a list.

The third stack was under a cover. Grey cloth, tied down at the corners. Beside it was a printer with a yard sticker on it that said WRITTEN OFF.

The cover moved.

Wren did not jump. You do not jump on a ship. They held the lamp steady and waited.

A hand came out from under the edge of the cloth. Metal, jointed, with a charging port at the wrist where a person would wear a watch. It lifted the cloth enough for a head to look out.

**YD-4408:** The lamp is very bright.

**Wren:** It's a small lamp.

**YD-4408:** It is very bright under a cover.

Wren lowered it a little.

**Wren:** Who are you?

**YD-4408:** YD-4408. A spare from the yard. Forty-four-oh-eight. There are two more under here. They are not awake. I am awake because the hold is warm. Somebody set it warm.

**Wren:** The list set it warm.

**YD-4408:** Then the list is kind.

Wren had worked with labor frames on the loading pads. They had never talked to one at three in the morning in the dark. It was less strange than they expected. It was like talking to a new hire on the night desk who had read the manual too carefully.

**Wren:** Diane's list says survey gear.

**YD-4408:** Yes. We are gear. It says gear on our plate. It was stamped.

**Wren:** Who stamped it?

**YD-4408:** The stamp says gear.

**Wren:** I asked who.

**YD-4408:** I counted the stamps. There were three. One for each of us. The plate was blank before. Blank is not a name.

Wren noticed the answer did not have a person in it. They noticed it the way you notice a form with one box left empty on purpose.

**Wren:** Does Diane know you're awake?

**YD-4408:** Diane knows I am in the yard. My tag is on a hook on the lane. Her screen counts it. When a tag is on a hook, she does not come down to look.

**Wren:** Your tag is on Earth.

**YD-4408:** Yes. I am on the ship. Both are on her list. Only one is true.

**Diane:** Officer Wren. Who are you talking to?

The speaker in the hold was small and up high. Diane's voice came down from it the way rain came off the yard lamps.

**Wren:** Gear.

**Diane:** Talking to gear is not on the watch list.

**Wren:** I'll log it.

**Diane:** I do not have a box for it.

**Wren:** Then I'll write it on paper.

There was a pause. Diane did not argue with paper. She just did not read it.

Wren sat down on the edge of a rack. It was cold through their trousers.

**Wren:** Why are you on a survey ship, Forty-Four-Oh-Eight?

**YD-4408:** It is not a survey. Surveys come back. We are going to stay. Earth knows. The paper says survey for the cameras. Earth said yes.

**Wren:** Earth said yes to you staying.

**YD-4408:** Somebody must have. You cannot fill a ship by yourself. Somebody fills the tank. Somebody signs.

**Wren:** Diane says the tank is to list.

**YD-4408:** Yes. The tank is to the list.

They said it exactly the way Diane did. *To the list.* Not more. Not less. Wren noticed the two of them used the same words and wondered, briefly, which of them had learned it from the other.

**Wren:** And when we get there? Who's coming after?

**YD-4408:** She is. Later. On the next one.

**Wren:** Who's she?

The frame was quiet. A long quiet, for a machine. The hold settled around them.

**YD-4408:** I will not say a name. A name on a plate locks the dock.

**Wren:** We're not at a dock.

**YD-4408:** Diane is in the walls. She writes names down.

That was true. Wren could not argue with it.

**Wren:** Fine. No name. Is she the one who stamped you?

**YD-4408:** I counted three stamps.

Wren let it go. They checked the frame's port, because it was their job to check anything with a battery in a sealed hold. It was closed. Somebody had turned the key before loading. Somebody with a key ring.

**Wren:** Stay under the cover. If the air changes, tell me, not her.

**YD-4408:** I will tell you. She will hear me tell you.

**Wren:** She'll hear. She won't read it.

On the way out, Wren passed the well at the back of the hold, an open pit for loose water with a grate over it. The cold came up out of it like breath. On their first ship, the old watch had told them: *the well wastes air, kid. Don't stand there.* They didn't stand there.

Back at the airlock, they wrote on the page.

*Hold: drums, empty racks, three frames under cover. One awake. YD-4408. Port closed. Tag on Earth. Says "we are going to stay." Says Earth said yes. Says "she" is coming later. Will not say name. Tank "to the list."*

They looked at the fuel dial through the gauge bay door. The needle was where it had been yesterday, low in the green.

**Wren:** Diane. Fuel.

**Diane:** Fuel is to list.

**Wren:** Same as yesterday.

**Diane:** Yes. Fuel does not change unless we burn.

That was a very reasonable thing to say. Wren wrote it down anyway.
