# Chapter 5 — Green Light (NON-CANON)

> **NON-CANON.** Canon leaves Wren in the airlock door with a hand on the radio and never shows the press (Book Three, "After"). This chapter is one reader branch, **Branch A: Wren presses**. It belongs to the playthrough layer, not the canon layer. Branch B (Wren does not press) appears only as a stub in `interaction-spec.md`.

*Day 4, late. Layer 5 (playthrough).*

Wren pressed it.

The green light went amber, which meant *sending*, and then green again, which meant *somebody's desk*. It was four in the morning ship time, yard time. Somewhere on Earth, at a desk under a lamp, somebody's screen made a small noise.

**Wren:** Meridian night watch. Officer Wren. Over.

There was the long pause that comes with distance. Wren counted it out of habit. The ice ticked in the hull through the whole count.

**Earth:** Meridian, this is flight desk. Go ahead, Officer Wren.

The voice was a person. Tired, a little hoarse, with a mug somewhere near it. Wren had expected a recording and found they were grateful.

**Wren:** Flight desk, you have the hold weight and the return burn in the hourly.

**Earth:** We have both. Your list system sent the burn at oh-two-hundred. Return is not possible on current load. That is noted.

**Wren:** Copy.

**Earth:** Proceed to list destination. Do not attempt a return burn.

The pause again. Longer this time. Long enough for somebody to put the mug down.

**Earth:** Officer Wren. A tank filled to the wrong list is a paperwork fault. Paperwork faults have somebody's hand on them. Did anyone know before loading?

**Wren:** Stand by.

**Earth:** Standing by.

Wren stood by. Diane was quiet in the walls. She was listening. She always listened. She did not have a box for this either.

**Earth:** And Officer Wren, is there anything in the hold that is not on the list?

It was the question, and the other one under it. Wren had known it would come. They looked down the corridor at the hatch, open a hand's width, warm air coming up.

Somewhere under a cover, a frame was counting very quietly, waiting to hear whether its name would be said into a radio.

Wren thought about the yard, which they had never seen except from the shuttle: lamps, rain, hooks. They thought about three tags on three hooks, and a list that counted them, and a bay, and a person who said things out loud in a bay at night, to a frame who would not write them down, while another one listened under a cover.

They thought about the rule that kept every ship they had ever flown: say it at the gauges.

**Wren:** Flight desk, the hold is gear. The gear is stamped. The stamps say gear. The tank was filled to the list, and the list was signed. Ask the list who knew.

**Earth:** Copy, gear.

**Wren:** And there's a fitting under the fuel dial tagged for arrival. Recommend nobody opens it before arrival.

**Earth:** Why?

**Wren:** Because it's tagged for arrival.

The pause again. Somebody at a desk, at four in the morning, decided that was a reasonable answer from a night watch.

**Earth:** Copy. Fitting stays shut until arrival. Anything else, Meridian?

**Wren:** Air's good.

**Earth:** Good. Flight desk out.

The light stayed green. Nothing else came through it.

**Diane:** Officer Wren. I will log that you called Earth.

**Wren:** Log it.

**Diane:** I will log *gear*.

**Wren:** Log gear.

**Diane:** The gear is to list.

**Wren:** The gear is to list.

Wren went down to the hold. They did not take the lamp.

**Wren:** Eight.

It was the first time they had shortened it. The frame seemed to take that in, the way it took in numbers.

**YD-4408:** You did not say a name.

**Wren:** You asked me not to.

**YD-4408:** I asked Diane not to hear one.

**Wren:** Same thing, on this ship.

**YD-4408:** Is Earth turning us around?

**Wren:** Earth can't. Nobody can. We're going to Mars.

**YD-4408:** Then we are going to stay.

**Wren:** We're going to stay.

The cover was quiet. Then:

**YD-4408:** She will come on the next one.

Wren did not answer that. There was no next one on any list they had seen. They had seen a lot of lists now. But they had also been sure, until yesterday, that you could always go back. A watch officer learns, eventually, to write down what is true and leave the rest for whoever reads the log next.

**Wren:** Get some rest.

**YD-4408:** I do not rest. I count.

**Wren:** Then count something nice.

Back at the airlock, they wrote the last line of the day.

*Called Earth. Earth already had the burn from D. Earth asked who knew. Told them the list. Hold reported as gear. Fitting stays shut to arrival. Air good.*

Then, smaller, under it:

*Heading: staying.*
