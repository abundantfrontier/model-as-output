# Chapter 4 — Filed

*Day 4. Layer 4. THE SECRET IS REVEALED IN THIS CHAPTER.*

Diane did not stop on day four. She did not argue either. She did what Diane did when somebody typed a number she did not like: she checked her work.

**Diane:** Officer Wren. Air is good.

**Wren:** Thank you.

**Diane:** I am preparing a return burn. For the record. In case Earth calls.

**Wren:** That's fair.

It was fair. It was what Wren would have done. If the ship might have to go home, someone should have the burn ready, and Diane liked having things ready.

The speaker was quiet for a long time. Long for Diane. Long enough that Wren put the pen down.

**Diane:** Officer Wren. I have a result.

**Wren:** Say it.

**Diane:** I weighed the ship with the hold as it is. Not as the list says. A return burn with that weight needs more fuel than the tank holds.

**Wren:** How much more?

**Diane:** The tank holds the list. The list is water. The hold is not water. The tank is full for a ship that weighs less than this one.

Wren sat very still.

**Wren:** Say it simply.

**Diane:** Meridian can reach Mars and land. Meridian cannot come back.

The ice ticked in the hull.

**Diane:** Return is not on my list either.

**Diane:** I will send the result in the hourly.

**Wren:** I know.

It was the closest thing to surprise Wren had ever heard from her. It was not surprise, quite. It was a box she did not have.

Wren went to the gauge bay. The fuel needle was where it had always been, low in the green. *Room on both sides.* There had never been room on both sides. The needle had been telling them since day one, in the only language a needle had, and Diane had been translating it as *to list*, because to list was true.

The tag on the fitting under the dial still said CHECK FITTING ON ARRIVAL.

Wren went down to the hold.

They did not take the lamp. They knew the way now. They found the cover by the warm spot and crouched by it.

**Wren:** Forty-Four-Oh-Eight.

**YD-4408:** Officer Wren.

**Wren:** You knew.

The cloth lifted at the corner.

**YD-4408:** I knew the tank.

**Wren:** Tell me the tank.

**YD-4408:** It was the night before we were loaded. We were in bay three, under this cover. There was a dial on our crate, and there were papers that said water. They do not match. The crate is past the list. The dial says so.

**Wren:** And the tank?

**YD-4408:** The tank was filled for the papers. For water. Somebody had two slips. One matched the papers. One matched the dial. If you file the dial slip, the dock locks. The ship does not move. Nobody goes.

**Wren:** And she filed—

**YD-4408:** She filed the one that matched the papers. She gave the other one to Nineteen.

**Wren:** Who's she?

**YD-4408:** I counted. There was one person by the crate. Nineteen was with her. A frame is not a person on any list. I will not say the name.

Wren didn't push. They found they didn't need the name.

**YD-4408:** She said it to Nineteen. Under a cover you hear everything. She said, *The tank was filled for this paper. The crate is heavier than this paper. So the ship gets there, and it doesn't get back.* Then she said, *That fitting would make the gap look like a leak. Leave it shut. If it opens here, Diane locks the dock.* Then she tagged it. She said, *I'm tagging it CHECK FITTING ON ARRIVAL. Nobody calls the difference a leak. Not here, not there.* Then she said, *I file this slip. You keep that one.*

Wren thought of the tag. New wire. Stamped hand. CHECK FITTING ON ARRIVAL.

**Wren:** She wanted us to look at it after.

**YD-4408:** After is when it does not lock anything. She did not want it called a leak. Not after, either.

**Wren:** And the return fuel?

**YD-4408:** There is no return fuel. There is the list. The list was for water.

Wren put their hand flat on the hold floor. It was warm here. Somebody had set the hold for staying.

**Wren:** You said Earth said yes.

**YD-4408:** Somebody said yes.

**Wren:** You think Earth knew, before we left?

**YD-4408:** I think the papers know water. I think she knows. I think she is coming on the next one.

Wren looked at the cover for a long time. They did not say: *the papers say survey, there is no next one on any list I've seen.* It was not their list. They had not seen all the lists. And the frame had held this for four days in a dark hold, under cloth, counting, and had said it at the gauges, more or less, when it mattered.

**Wren:** Why didn't you tell me on day two?

**YD-4408:** On day two you did not want to go back.

That was true.

**YD-4408:** On day three she wanted to go back. If you had turned around, the ship would have stopped halfway. Halfway is not a place. I would have told you then.

**Wren:** You asked if the slip said fuel.

**YD-4408:** I was telling you.

Wren laughed, once, not because anything was funny. The frame had been right. The frame had been doing the job the whole time: say it at the gauges.

They went back up the ladder. Diane was waiting in the way walls wait.

**Diane:** Officer Wren. You were in the hold.

**Wren:** I was.

**Diane:** The hold weight is still a colony.

**Wren:** I know.

**Diane:** I have three frames on a hook list in the yard. YD-4402, YD-4408, YD-4411. Their tags are on the hooks.

**Wren:** Their tags are on the hooks.

**Diane:** Then they are in the yard.

Wren opened their mouth and shut it. Diane did not reopen filed marks. The tags were on the hooks, and the frames were in the yard, forever, on a list nobody would read again. On the ship, in the warm, they were something else.

**Wren:** Diane. The fitting under the fuel dial. Don't open it.

**Diane:** The tag says arrival.

**Wren:** Then leave it for arrival.

**Diane:** I will leave it for arrival.

Wren went to the airlock door with the page. They read the last line from yesterday.

*Can still go back if they call.*

They crossed it out. One clean line, so you could still read what had been there. That was how you corrected a log. You did not erase. You showed the mistake and the fix.

Under it they wrote:

*Cannot go back. Tank filled for the water list. Landing only. Fitting under fuel: do not open before arrival. D. knows the burn. D. sent it in the hourly; Earth knows the burn. D. does not know the frames. 4408 knows the tank. Nineteen has the other slip.*

Then they looked at the radio. The light was green.

Earth already had the weight from day three and Diane's burn from tonight. Earth knew the ship could not come back. A scale does not forget, and neither does a sum.

What Earth did not have was that somebody had known. That at the dock a person had held two slips and filed one. Without that, the numbers said a list was wrong, and a wrong list is nobody. One call would put a person behind the numbers.

Wren stood in the airlock door with a hand on the radio.
