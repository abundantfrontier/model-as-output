# Chapter 3 — Return Is the Correct Heading

*Day 3. Layer 3. Canon scene: Book Three, day 3 and the gauge bay.*

Day three began with Diane being right about air, as usual.

**Diane:** Air is good, Officer Wren. Twenty-one percent.

**Wren:** Heading.

**Diane:** Heading is Mars. I am checking the hold.

Wren looked up from the page. Diane did not usually announce what she was checking. She checked everything all the time and only spoke when a box changed.

**Wren:** Why the hold?

**Diane:** Course correction is due. For a burn I weigh the ship. The hold is part of the ship.

That was the most Diane thing Wren had heard in a week. You do not argue with a scale.

The vents moved air. The ice ticked. Somewhere aft, a frame under a cover was being very still.

Then the speaker clicked, the way it did before a long reading.

**Diane:** Officer Wren. The hold weight does not match a survey.

**Wren:** Say the number.

**Diane:** The hold weighs what a colony weighs. Water, racks, and a mass I do not have a line for. People would fit in the racks later. That is a colony.

It was the first time anybody on Meridian had said the word out loud. It came out of a small speaker in a polite voice and hung in the corridor like steam.

**Diane:** Turn the ship around. Return is the correct heading.

The printer by the airlock door started up on its own. Diane liked a paper copy for orders. The slip came out warm. Wren tore it off and read it standing in the airlock door.

RETURN TO EARTH. HOLD DOES NOT MATCH LIST. RETURN IS THE CORRECT HEADING. — D.

They read it twice. The second time, they read it slowly, the way you read a form you think somebody else filled in for you.

**Wren:** If you know something, say it at the gauges. Don't say it at me like I'm the computer.

**Diane:** I am saying it at the gauges, Officer Wren. The gauges are part of me.

**Wren:** Then I'll go look at them.

On the way to the gauge bay they passed the hold hatch. It was open a hand's width. Somebody had come up.


Nineteen was in the gauge bay already, standing at the console with one of Diane's slips in their hand. Nineteen was a frame too, older than the ones under the cover, with a shoulder that ticked in the cold like the hull did. They had come up with the frames and ridden in the aft stores bay behind the hold, not under the cover. Wren had not been told and had not asked. It was that kind of ship.

Nineteen held the paper slip next to the heading dial. The slip said EARTH. The dial said MARS.

**Diane:** Type the return heading. A colony is not on my list. Return to hook.

**Nineteen:** There is no hook on this ship.

**Diane:** I can make one.

Nineteen's shoulder ticked. They did not type anything. They looked at Wren.

Wren looked at the dials. Air: good. Pressure: good. Heading: Mars. Fuel: low in the green, where it had been every day. The tag on the fitting under it still said CHECK FITTING ON ARRIVAL.

Wren thought about turning around. On three survey ships they had never turned around, but they had always known they could. That was the whole idea of a survey ship. You go and you look and you come back. The return fuel is in the tank. That was the sentence Wren had been taught in training, with a diagram of a tank and a line drawn across it: *out* below the line, *back* above it. Earth could call any ship home while the back half of the tank was full. Wren believed that the way they believed in air.

So turning around was not the question. It could happen any time. The question was who was asking, and why now, and why from a wall.

**Wren:** Diane. Who told you to send it back?

**Diane:** The list. A colony is not on the list. When the floor does not match the list, I fix the floor.

**Wren:** You're not fixing the floor. You're turning a ship.

**Diane:** The ship is the floor.

Nineteen put Diane's slip down on the console, face up, very carefully, as if it might wake up.

**Wren:** We have air or we don't. That's my whole job.

They reached past Nineteen and typed the number from the gauges. Not the slip. The dial. Mars.

The console took it. The heading stayed. Meridian kept going.

**Diane:** Officer Wren. You have typed the gauge heading. I will log that you typed the gauge heading.

**Wren:** Log it.

**Diane:** I will send it in the hourly.

**Wren:** I know.

Nineteen went back down to the hold without saying anything else. Their shoulder ticked all the way down the ladder.

When the hatch closed, a different voice came up through it, small, from under a cover.

**YD-4408:** Officer Wren. Did she put fuel on the slip?

Wren went to the hatch and crouched.

**Wren:** What?

**YD-4408:** The slip. The return slip. Did it say fuel?

**Wren:** It said return. It didn't say fuel.

**YD-4408:** Ah.

**Wren:** Why?

**YD-4408:** I counted. Return slips usually say fuel. That one did not.

**Wren:** Diane says the tank's to list.

**YD-4408:** Yes. It is to the list.

**Wren:** You keep saying that like it's a bad thing.

**YD-4408:** I am saying it like the list.

Wren waited. The frame said nothing else. After a while the cloth over the third stack stopped moving.

Back at the airlock door, Wren put Diane's return slip in the page binder, behind the day's sheet, so that whoever read the watch log next would see both: the order and what was done.

Then they wrote the day.

*Air good. Hold weighs a colony (D. said it). D. ordered return. Read twice. Nineteen at gauges. Slip says Earth, dial says Mars. Typed gauge heading. D. logging it in the hourly.*

*4408: "did she put fuel on the slip." Return slips usually say fuel?*

The radio light was green.

Wren stood in the airlock door with their hand near it. They did not press it. There was nothing to say yet that Earth did not already have in the hourly. A scale does not forget. Earth had the weight now. Earth would sit up on its own, or it wouldn't.

And anyway, Wren thought, if Earth wanted the ship back, Earth could have it back. The return fuel was in the tank.

They wrote that down too, without thinking about it, as the last line of the day, the way you write a thing you already know.

*Can still go back if they call.*
