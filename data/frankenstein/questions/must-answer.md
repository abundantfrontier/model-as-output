# Must-answer questions

Gist answers for a reader who has finished the named unit and no further. The 1818 text is the only source.

## m01 — vol1-letter-1 (position 1)

Who is writing the first letter, and to whom?

Robert Walton writes to his sister, Mrs Saville, in England. The letter is dated 11 December from St. Petersburgh. He signs later letters R. W.

Source: Volume I, Letter I, the address and the opening.

## m02 — vol1-letter-1 (position 1)

What does Walton expect the North Pole to be like?

He refuses the idea that the pole is only frost and desolation. He pictures it as a region of beauty and delight, with the sun always visible, and he wants the glory of getting there.

Source: Volume I, Letter I.

## m03 — vol1-letter-2 (position 2)

What does Walton say he still does not have, once the ship is hired?

A friend. He writes from Archangel on 28 March that he has hired a vessel and is gathering sailors, but there is no one to share success or disappointment. He is twenty-eight and calls himself self-educated.

Source: Volume I, Letter II.

## m04 — vol1-letter-3 (position 3)

By 7 July, what has gone wrong on the voyage?

Very little. Walton tells his sister he is safe and far north. There have been stiff gales, and a mast has broken. He promises to be cool and prudent. He has not yet met anyone on the ice.

Source: Volume I, Letter III.

## m05 — vol1-letter-4 (position 4)

What do Walton and the crew see on the ice, and whom do they bring aboard the next morning?

On 31 July they see a huge figure, shaped like a man, driving a dog-sledge north across the ice. The next morning a second sledge drifts alongside with one dog still alive. The man on it is a starving European. He asks where the ship is bound, and only then comes aboard. The letter calls him the stranger and does not give his name.

Source: Volume I, Letter IV, 5 August.

## m06 — vol1-letter-4 (position 4)

What does the stranger agree to tell Walton?

On 19 August he agrees to tell his history the next day. He says he has suffered unparalleled misfortunes, that he once sought knowledge as Walton does, and that he has lost everything, including a friend. He says his fate is nearly fulfilled. He has not yet begun the story.

Source: Volume I, Letter IV, 19 August.

## m07 — vol1-ch-1 (position 5)

Who is Elizabeth, in this book?

She is Victor's cousin, Elizabeth Lavenza. She is the daughter of his father's sister, who had married an Italian. After the sister's death the child is sent from Italy to be raised as Alphonse Frankenstein's own daughter. Caroline wants Elizabeth and Victor to marry. She is not a peasant child found by a lake.

Source: Volume I, Chapter I. This is the 1818 account, not the 1831 one.

## m08 — vol1-ch-1 (position 5)

How does Victor first get interested in science?

At thirteen, kept indoors by rain near Thonon, he finds Cornelius Agrippa. His father glances at the title and says it is sad trash, but he does not explain why, so Victor goes on to Paracelsus and Albertus Magnus. A later lightning strike, explained to him as electricity, knocks down his faith in those old writers.

Source: Volume I, Chapter I.

## m09 — vol1-ch-1 (position 5)

Who is Henry Clerval as a boy?

He is the son of a Geneva merchant and Victor's school friend. He loves books of chivalry and romance, writes a fairy tale at nine, and makes the children act plays about Orlando, Robin Hood, Amadis, and St George.

Source: Volume I, Chapter I.

## m10 — vol1-ch-2 (position 6)

How does Victor's mother die?

Elizabeth catches scarlet fever and is soon recovering. Caroline goes in to nurse her before the danger is past, sickens on the third day, and dies. On her deathbed she joins Victor's hand and Elizabeth's, says she had hoped they would marry, and asks Elizabeth to take her place with the younger cousins.

Source: Volume I, Chapter II.

## m11 — vol1-ch-2 (position 6)

What is the difference between Professor Krempe and Professor Waldman?

Both teach at Ingolstadt. M. Krempe, professor of natural philosophy, is rude about Cornelius Agrippa and the old alchemists. M. Waldman, who lectures on chemistry, speaks of those same writers without contempt and offers a modern science that can do what they only dreamed of. Victor leaves Waldman's lecture as his disciple. He says that day decided his destiny.

Source: Volume I, Chapter II.

## m12 — vol1-ch-3 (position 7)

What question takes over Victor's studies before the night the creature wakes?

He asks where the principle of life comes from. He studies decay, decides he has found how to animate lifeless matter, and begins the work. The eye has not yet opened. That night is the next chapter.

Source: Volume I, Chapter III.

## m13 — vol1-ch-4 (position 8)

What happens on the night the creature comes to life?

It is a dreary night in November, about one in the morning, and rain hits the window. The yellow eye opens and the body breathes. Victor had chosen the features to be beautiful: lustrous black hair, white teeth. He is horrified by the watery eyes, the shrivelled yellow skin, and the straight black lips. He runs.

Source: Volume I, Chapter IV, the opening.

## m14 — vol1-ch-4 (position 8)

Who finds Victor after that night, and does Victor tell him the truth?

Henry Clerval finds him in Ingolstadt and greets him as Frankenstein. Victor collapses into a long illness. He does not tell Clerval what he made.

Source: Volume I, Chapter IV.

## m15 — vol1-ch-5 (position 9)

Who is Justine Moritz, the first time the reader meets her?

Elizabeth's letter introduces her. She is the daughter of Madame Moritz, who disliked her. Caroline took her into the Frankenstein house at twelve and educated her. She nursed Caroline in her last illness, went home when her own mother was dying, and has now returned. In this letter she is loved and safe, and William is alive.

Source: Volume I, Chapter V, Elizabeth's letter.

## m16 — vol1-ch-6 (position 10)

How does Victor learn that William is dead?

His father writes that William was murdered on the evening of 7 May. The family had been walking at Plainpalais. William ran off to hide, and was later found dead. He had been wearing a miniature of his mother.

Source: Volume I, Chapter VI, Alphonse's letter.

## m17 — vol1-ch-6 (position 10)

Whom does Victor believe killed William, and why, before anyone confesses?

In a storm at the place of the murder, a flash of lightning shows him the creature he made. He becomes convinced that the creature is the murderer. Justine is accused because the miniature was found on her. Victor says she is innocent. He does not yet have the creature's own account.

Source: Volume I, Chapter VI.

## m18 — vol1-ch-7 (position 11)

What happens at Justine's trial?

Justine is tried for William's murder. Elizabeth speaks for her and says the miniature had been Justine's only because William asked to wear it. The ballots are all black. Justine had confessed under pressure from her confessor, then tells Elizabeth and Victor that she is innocent. She is executed. Victor stays silent about the creature.

Source: Volume I, Chapter VII.

## m19 — vol2-ch-1 (position 12)

What state is Victor in after Justine's death, before he meets the creature on the glacier?

He is alive and full of remorse. He avoids people, sleeps badly, and feels that he himself is guilty of the deaths. The family travels toward the Alps hoping the change will help him. He has not yet met the creature again.

Source: Volume II, Chapter I.

## m20 — vol2-ch-2 (position 13)

Where does Victor meet the creature again, and what does the creature claim?

Victor climbs Montanvert and goes down onto the sea of ice. The creature finds him there. He says Victor owes him a duty, that he should have been Adam but has been treated as a fallen angel, and that he was benevolent and good until misery made him a fiend. If Victor will hear his conditions, he will leave mankind in peace. He does not yet say what the conditions are.

Source: Volume II, Chapter II.

## m21 — vol2-ch-3 (position 14)

How does the creature describe the beginning of his life?

He says it is hard to remember the first era of his being. He came to know light, dark, hunger, cold, and the sounds of birds and streams with no one to teach him. He does not yet tell the cottagers' story.

Source: Volume II, Chapter III, the opening of his narrative.

## m22 — vol2-ch-4 (position 15)

Why does the creature stop taking the cottagers' food?

He has been watching a blind old man and two younger people in a poor cottage, and learning words from them. He sees that when he takes their food the younger ones go hungry, so he stops. He wants to be kind to people who do not yet know he is there.

Source: Volume II, Chapter IV.

## m23 — vol2-ch-5 (position 16)

Who is Safie, and how does the creature learn faster because of her?

Safie arrives at the cottage and does not speak the family's language. Felix teaches her. The creature studies with them from hiding. One book Felix uses is Volney's Ruins of Empires. The creature learns the words, and also hears about conquest and suffering.

Source: Volume II, Chapter V.

## m24 — vol2-ch-6 (position 17)

Who are the cottagers, and why are they in exile?

The old man is De Lacey (the 1818 text also spells the name De Lacy). Felix and Agatha are his children. They had been prosperous in Paris. Felix helped Safie's father, a Turkish merchant, out of prison, and the family was ruined for it. Safie's mother was a Christian Arab. Safie later travels to find Felix.

Source: Volume II, Chapter VI.

## m25 — vol2-ch-7 (position 18)

What does the creature read, and what happens when he finally enters the cottage?

He reads Paradise Lost, Plutarch's Lives, and The Sorrows of Werter, and he reads Victor's laboratory journal from a pocket of the coat he took. He goes in while De Lacey is alone, because the old man cannot see his face, and is received kindly. Felix, Safie, and Agatha return. Agatha faints, Safie runs, and Felix beats him to the ground. He does not fight back. He escapes. He has not yet told of burning the cottage or of William.

Source: Volume II, Chapter VII.

## m26 — vol2-ch-8 (position 19)

What does the creature do after the family leaves the cottage?

Felix says they can never live there again. The creature burns the cottage. Later he pulls a girl from a rapid stream and a rustic shoots him. About two months after that he reaches Geneva, seizes a child who says his father is a Frankenstein, and strangles him. The child is William. He puts the portrait from the child's breast into the dress of a young woman who passes by, so the blame will fall on her.

Source: Volume II, Chapter VIII, the creature's own account.

## m27 — vol2-ch-9 (position 20)

What does the creature demand, and what does Victor promise?

A female creature, to live with him away from people. Victor refuses, then agrees, on the creature's oath to leave Europe and the neighbourhood of mankind as soon as she is delivered. The creature swears by the sun and the blue sky of heaven, and leaves across the sea of ice.

Source: Volume II, Chapter IX.

## m28 — vol3-ch-1 (position 21)

Does Victor start the second creature as soon as he is home?

No. Back in Geneva he delays, week after week. He dreads the creature and is disgusted by the work. He has heard of an English philosopher whose discoveries he thinks he needs, and he considers asking his father to let him visit England, but he keeps putting it off.

Source: Volume III, Chapter I.

## m29 — vol3-ch-2 (position 22)

Why are Victor and Clerval in London?

Victor is collecting what he needs to finish the companion, using letters of introduction to natural philosophers. Clerval wants the company of talented men. Victor finds ordinary cheerful company painful. The female creature has not been destroyed yet.

Source: Volume III, Chapter II.

## m30 — vol3-ch-3 (position 23)

What does Victor do on the Orkney island, and what does the creature say as he leaves?

He looks at the unfinished female and thinks she might refuse the exile, hate her mate, or be worse than he is. He destroys the work. The creature, who has followed him, says he will be with Victor on his wedding night, then leaves in his boat. The wedding has not happened, and the chapter does not say whom the threat names.

Source: Volume III, Chapter III.

## m31 — vol3-ch-4 (position 24)

What is Victor accused of in Ireland?

Clerval is found dead. Victor is brought before a magistrate and charged. A jury later rejects the bill when it is shown that Victor was on the Orkney island at the time. He does not tell the court about the creature. Elizabeth is still alive.

Source: Volume III, Chapter IV.

## m32 — vol3-ch-5 (position 25)

How does Victor leave Ireland?

His father has come to him. They sail from Ireland. Victor will not go back through London, because he cannot bear the places where he was briefly at peace with Clerval. His father thinks the shame of the charge is the wound. The wedding has not yet happened.

Source: Volume III, Chapter V.

## m33 — vol3-ch-6 (position 26)

What happens on the wedding night, and what happens to Victor's father?

Victor, expecting the attack on himself, sends Elizabeth to her room and searches the house. He hears her scream. She is dead across the bed, the mark of the creature's grip on her neck. The creature grins at the open window and points at her. Victor fires. The creature leaps into the lake and escapes. Victor returns to Geneva. His father dies of an apoplectic fit in his arms a few days later. Ernest is still alive. Victor himself is not dead.

Source: Volume III, Chapter VI.

## m34 — vol3-ch-7 (position 27)

How does the book end?

Victor hunts the creature across the ice and is taken aboard Walton's ship. He dies after telling Walton to seek happiness in tranquillity and to avoid ambition, even the ambition to distinguish himself in science. The creature comes aboard, mourns over the body, and says he will burn himself on a funeral pile at the northern extremity so that no one can make another like him. He springs from the cabin window onto the ice raft and is lost in darkness and distance.

Source: Volume III, Chapter VII, Walton's continuation and the last scene.
